# The Tartaro

- **Tradition:** Basque people (Europe)
- **Source book:** *Basque Legends; With an Essay on the Basque Language*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Webster, Wentworth (author, d. 1907)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #34902 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34902
- **Copyright status:** Public domain worldwide: the text was published long ago and every named author/translator died in or before 1955.

---

Once upon a time there was the son of a king who for the punishment of some fault became a monster. He could become a man again only by marrying. One day he met a young girl who refused him, because she was so frightened at him. And the Tartaro wanted to give her a ring, which she would not accept. However, he sent it her by a young man. As soon as the ring was upon her finger it began to say, “Thou there, and I here.” It kept always crying out this, and the Tartaro pursued her continually; and, as the young girl had such a horror of him, she cut off her finger and the ring, and threw them into a large pond, and there the Tartaro drowned himself.

Estefanella Hirigarray , of Ahetze.
