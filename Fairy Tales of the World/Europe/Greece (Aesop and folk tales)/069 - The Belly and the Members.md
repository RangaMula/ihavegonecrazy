# The Belly and the Members

- **Tradition:** Greece (Aesop and folk tales) (Europe)
- **Source book:** *Aesop's Fables
Translated by George Fyler Townsend*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Aesop (author); Townsend, George Fyler (translator, d. 1900)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #21 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/21
- **Copyright status:** Public domain in the USA (Project Gutenberg). The underlying folk tale is traditional and free for anyone; this particular English wording's status outside the USA depends on the translator's death date, which is not recorded.

---

THE MEMBERS of the Body rebelled against the Belly, and said, “Why should we be perpetually engaged in administering to your wants, while you do nothing but take your rest, and enjoy yourself in luxury and self-indulgence?” The Members carried out their resolve and refused their assistance to the Belly. The whole Body quickly became debilitated, and the hands, feet, mouth, and eyes, when too late, repented of their folly.
