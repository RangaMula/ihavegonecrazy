# The St. Levan Stone

- **Tradition:** Cornwall (Cornish people) (Europe)
- **Source book:** *Traditions and Hearthside Stories of West Cornwall, Second Series*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Bottrell, William (author, d. 1881)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #41761 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/41761 (copied via the GITenberg mirror)
- **Copyright status:** Public domain worldwide: the text was published long ago and every named author/translator died in or before 1955.

---

In St. Levan Churchyard is a cloven rock called St. Levan's stone. For some reason, now unknown, this must have been a venerated object when the church was built, or it would have been used in the building. The common notion, however, is that long before St. Levan's time this rock was regarded as sacred, because Merlin prophecied—

"When, with panniers astride, A pack-horse one can ride Through St. Levan stone, The world will be done."

It is stated that Merlin came here with King Arthur, when he slaughtered the Danes at the battle of Velan-druchar. The separation of this prophetic stone is so slow that there appears to be no danger of the world's ending just yet.
