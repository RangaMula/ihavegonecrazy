# A Legend of Kandy

- **Tradition:** Sri Lanka (Sinhalese people) (Asia)
- **Source book:** *Village Folk-Tales of Ceylon, Volume 2 (of 3)*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Parker, H. (Henry) (translator)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #57399 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/57399
- **Copyright status:** Public domain in the USA (Project Gutenberg). The underlying folk tale is traditional and free for anyone; this particular English wording's status outside the USA depends on the translator's death date, which is not recorded.

At a certain place in Lan̥kāwa (Ceylon), there was an extensive forest. In that forest there were elephants, bears, leopards, wan̆durās, and many other jungle animals.

At any time whatever, at the time when any animal springs for seizing an animal that is its prey, it comes running near a rock that is in an open place in the forest. Having arrived near the rock, the animal that ran through fear goes bounding back after the animal that is chasing it. Regarding that rock, it was the custom that it was [known as] “The Rock of the Part where there is Tranquillity” ( Sen̥-kaḍa-gala ).

One day a Basket-mender for the purpose of cutting bamboos went into this forest. While he was cutting bamboos a certain jackal went driving a hare on the path. At the time when the hare arrived near this rock the jackal began to run back, and the hare ran behind it.

The Basket-mender, having been looking at this, examined the place, and having gone near the King who was ruling at that time, told him of this circumstance. And the King, having thought that it is a good victorious ground, went there, and having built a city makes it his capital ( rāja-dhāniya ). For that city he made the name Sen̥kaḍagala [Nuwara—that is, Kandy].

Ūva Province.

* * *

The Sinhalese title is, “The Jackal and the Basket-mender,”—at least this is what I take to be the meaning of Kulupottā , a word I do not know, deriving pottā from the Tamil pottu , to mend; compare Kuḷuyara , a basket-maker.  ↑

A large monkey of two species ( Semnopithecus ).  ↑

Deriving Seṇ from sema . Kandy appears to have been founded at the beginning of the fourteenth century ( Ancient Ceylon , p. 354, note).  ↑
