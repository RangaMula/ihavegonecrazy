# The Kaḍambāwa Men and the Bush

- **Tradition:** Sri Lanka (Sinhalese people) (Asia)
- **Source book:** *Village Folk-Tales of Ceylon, Volume 1 (of 3)*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Parker, H. (Henry) (translator)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #56614 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/56614
- **Copyright status:** Public domain in the USA (Project Gutenberg). The underlying folk tale is traditional and free for anyone; this particular English wording's status outside the USA depends on the translator's death date, which is not recorded.

As the Kaḍambāwa men were going away with some drums one night, to attend a devil-dance, they met with a Warā bush on the path, which looked like an elephant. The men became afraid, thinking, “Maybe an elephant has come onto the path.” At the shaking of the leaves of the Warā bush they said, “He is shaking his ears.”

Being afraid to go past the elephant, they beat the drums until it became light, to frighten the Warā bush. When they looked after it became light, it was not an elephant; it was a Warā tree. After that, they came back to their village. So they had neither the devil-dance nor went to sleep.
