# Wiske·djak’s Love Affair

- **Tradition:** Ojibwe and Algonquin peoples (Americas)
- **Source book:** *Myths and Folk-lore of the Timiskaming Algonquin and Timagami Ojibwa*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Speck, F. G. (author, d. 1950)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #66509 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/66509 (copied via the GITenberg mirror)
- **Copyright status:** Public domain worldwide: the text was published long ago and every named author/translator died in or before 1955.

---

Wiske·djak never got married to a woman. But he pretended to get married. One time he dressed himself like a woman, with skirts, and tried to deceive a young man, so that he thought Wiske·djak was his wife. Wiske·djak pretended to be jealous of the man when he went away. The other people knew that it was Wiske·djak all the time and laughed to themselves and made fun of the pair. The young man lived with Wiske·djak for some time, thinking it was his wife. But the other people made fun of the young man so much that at last he left Wiske·djak. Several times he got young men to live with him as their wife, but at last they all left him and he went away by himself. He was a queer fellow, that Wiske·djak. He never got married because he would not be bothered with a woman, as he had to be travelling all the time. [ ]
