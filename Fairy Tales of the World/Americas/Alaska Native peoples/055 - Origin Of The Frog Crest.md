# Origin Of The Frog Crest

- **Tradition:** Alaska Native peoples (Americas)
- **Source book:** *Myths and Legends of Alaska*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Judson, Katharine Berry (compiler, d. 1929)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #47146 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/47146
- **Copyright status:** Public domain worldwide: the text was published long ago and every named author/translator died in or before 1955.

Tlingit

L ONG ago, a man and his wife were crossing a large bay near Sitka when it became very foggy. They could not even see the water around their canoe. Then, in the thick fog, they heard singing. The song was:

The voice was so strong they could hear it reëcho among the mountains.

As the fog rose, the song came nearer and nearer. At last they saw the voice came from a wee little frog.

The man said: “This frog is mine. I shall claim it.”

His wife said, “No, it is mine. I shall claim it.”

Thus they argued. At last the man let his wife take the frog.

The woman took it ashore, treating it just like a child. She took it up into the woods, put it down by a lake, and left it there. That is why the Kiksadi clan at Sitka claim the frog as their totem.
