# How The Pheasant Beat Corn

- **Tradition:** Native North America (various nations) (Americas)
- **Source book:** *Myths and Legends of the Great Plains*
- **Author / collector / translator:** Judson, Katharine Berry (editor, d. 1929)
- **Original language of this text:** en
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #22083 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/22083 (copied via the GITenberg mirror)
- **Copyright status:** Public domain worldwide: the text was published long ago and every named author/translator died in or before 1955.

---

Cherokee

O nce Pheasant saw a woman beating corn in a wooden mortar in front of her lodge.

“I can do that, too,” said Pheasant.

“I don’t believe you,” said the woman.

“Yes, I can,” said Pheasant. So Pheasant went into the woods behind the lodge. He flew to a hollow log and drummed with his wings until the people thought he really was beating corn.

That is why the Indians have the Pheasant dance, as a part of the Green-corn dance.
