# Folk-heritage institutions by country (like Bangla Academy)

National academies, folklore archives and cultural institutes that collect and preserve each
country's oral tales. **This page lists links only. No text from these organisations was copied into
this collection**, because their publications are normally protected by copyright, even when they
are free to read online. Use them to find tales, then:

- copy a text only if the organisation states an open licence (CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA) or the text is
  in the public domain; or
- ask the organisation for permission (many are happy to help translators); or
- write your own retelling of the traditional plot, crediting the source. Plots are not copyrighted;
  the wording is.

Web addresses are from general knowledge and were **not checked from this session**. Confirm them before
citing.

## International
- **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage** — ich.unesco.org: lists of oral traditions by country.
- **StoryWeaver (Pratham Books)** — storyweaver.org.in: thousands of CC BY 4.0 children's books in 300+
  languages, including many folk tales (Bengali, Hindi, Indonesian, African and others). Reading the
  full text needs a free account, so they could not be collected automatically from this session.
- **Global Storybooks** — globalstorybooks.net: CC BY stories in many languages.
- **Wikisource** — public-domain texts in every language. Good for filling gaps, e.g. Bengali
  *Thakurmar Jhuli* (public domain in Bangladesh and India now, worldwide from 2028).
