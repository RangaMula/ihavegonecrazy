# Fairy Tales of the World — উপকথা ও রূপকথা

A collection of **4,608 traditional folk tales, fairy tales, fables and legends** from
**81 countries, regions and peoples (ethnic groups)**, sorted into one folder per tradition
(up to 100 tales each). Every tale is copied word for word from a **public-domain** book, so it can be
read, shared, adapted and **translated into any language** without needing anyone's permission.

Built on 8 November 2026.

## Folder layout

```
Fairy Tales of the World/
├── <Continent>/
│   └── <Country, region or people>/
│       ├── _README.md            ← sources, copyright status and list of tales for this folder
│       ├── 001 - <Title>.md      ← one tale per file, with a short source/copyright header
│       └── ...
├── index.csv                     ← every tale: continent, tradition, title, file, source, credits, status
└── _tools/                       ← the scripts used to build this collection (re-runnable)
```

## Why these tales are free of legal barriers

1. **The stories themselves** are traditional oral tales. Nobody owns a folk tale's plot or characters.
2. **The wording** comes from books digitised by Project Gutenberg (copied through its GitHub mirror,
   GITenberg). Gutenberg only publishes books that are public domain in the USA.
3. **Public domain worldwide (the strictest test):** most countries protect a text for the author's
   life plus 70 years. Every book used was checked against the death dates of its authors, collectors and
   translators. Illustrators don't count because no illustrations were copied.
   - Books where any named author or translator **died after 1955 were removed**. Examples: Josef Cohen's
     Dutch *Sagen en Legenden* (d. 1965), Elsie Spicer Eells's Brazilian tales (d. 1963), Mabel Powers's
     Iroquois stories, Maive Stokes's *Indian Fairy Tales* (d. 1961) and Ressler's *Treasury of American Indian Tales*.
   - **3,419 tales** are marked *Public domain worldwide*: every named contributor died in or before 1955.
   - **1,189 tales** are marked *Public domain in the USA*. Each was published long ago (most in the
     1800s or early 1900s), but Gutenberg's catalogue has no death date for one contributor, so the
     life+70 test could not be confirmed automatically. The traditional story is free either way. Only
     if you will publish this exact English wording in a life+70 country is it worth checking that one
     translator. Each tale's header says which case applies.
4. Project Gutenberg's licence and trademark text was removed. Only the public-domain story text
   remains, with the eBook number given as a citation.

## Ethical notes before translating

- These are **old texts (mostly 1850–1930)**. Some collectors' wording uses outdated or offensive terms for
  peoples (e.g. "Hottentot", "Eskimo", "Siwash") or a colonial point of view. Review and modernise the
  wording before using a tale with children.
- Many Indigenous stories (Native American, Aboriginal Australian, Māori, Hawaiian, San, etc.) were
  recorded by outsiders. Some are **sacred narratives** that their communities may want told in a
  particular way, at particular times, or credited to them. Name the people the story comes from. Where
  possible, ask present-day community organisations before publishing a retelling.
- Folder names use the people's own name first where possible, e.g. "Dakota-Lakota (Sioux)" or
  "Navajo (Diné)".

## Gaps (and how to fill them)

"100 tales for every country and people on Earth" cannot be met from texts that are free *everywhere*
today. For many traditions no older translation exists whose translator died before 1956, or the
surviving texts belong to living or recently deceased authors. Known gaps:

- **Thin folders** (fewer than 100 tales): the source books themselves are small. Every distinct tale
  found was included.
- **Ainu and Brazil** are empty. Chamberlain's *Aino Folk-Tales* (PD worldwide) is laid out as numbered
  paragraphs that the automatic splitter couldn't separate. The only Brazilian collections on Gutenberg
  are by Elsie Spicer Eells (d. 1963, still in copyright in life+70 countries until 2034).
- **Not yet covered**: much of Africa, Latin America, the Middle East, Central and South-East Asia,
  the Caribbean and the Pacific. Most collections for these regions were published after 1930 or by
  authors who died after 1955.
- **Bengali originals:** *Thakurmar Jhuli* (Dakshinaranjan Mitra Majumder, d. 1957) is public domain in
  Bangladesh and India (life + 60) but not in life + 70 countries until 1 January 2028. This collection
  includes Lal Behari Day's *Folk-Tales of Bengal* (1883) instead.
- The access used for this build reached Project Gutenberg only through its GitHub mirror. Wikisource and
  Internet Archive could fill more gaps (e.g. Andrew Lang's colour fairy books split by country,
  Basile's *Pentamerone*, Afanasyev in Russian, Grimm in German).

## Rebuilding

```bash
cd "Fairy Tales of the World/_tools"
python3 fetch_meta.py repos.tsv   # download Gutenberg metadata (needs access to raw.githubusercontent.com)
python3 build.py ../_rebuild      # split books into tales and write the folders
```
`catalog.py` holds the curated country/people → book mapping. `build.py` holds the copyright rules
(cut-off year, known death dates, excluded books).
