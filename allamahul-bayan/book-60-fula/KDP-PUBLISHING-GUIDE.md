   Fula has many regional varieties; this draft mixes Pular (Guinea) and Pulaar (Senegal) forms. A native speaker should check: (1) every Adlam spelling, especially the long-vowel marks, the gemination mark (used for every double consonant, e.g. alluwal, lekki, kammu) and prenasalised clusters (nd, ng: loonde, naange, weendu), which are written here as plain n + consonant; (2) the letter names (alif, daali, laam … taken from the Unicode names; teachers may say a, da, la … instead); (3) the words piindi (flowers), ñiiri (food), ɗerewol (paper), toɓo (rain), kaaƴe (rocks); (4) the phrases (Innde am ko …, yumma, musiɗɗo gorko/debbo, Miɗo jaŋnga Pulaar, A waɗii moƴƴa), the numbers (ɓolum for zero, go'o with the Adlam hamza) and the cover/title words (Alkule Adlam, Ndee deftere ko am, limre, konngi).
